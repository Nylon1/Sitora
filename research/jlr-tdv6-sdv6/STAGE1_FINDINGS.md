# Stage 1 Findings

## 1. Manufacturer technical knowledge

### SSM72578

Jaguar Land Rover Special Service Message **SSM72578**, preserved in the US NHTSA technical-bulletin archive, is titled **“Crankshaft/Crankshaft Bearing Concerns - 3.0 TDV6 Diesel Engine.”**

The document identifies:

- crankshaft/crankshaft-bearing failure;
- rotation of main-bearing shells;
- seized crankshaft;
- metallic debris;
- serious rumbling/knocking;
- oil-pressure warnings;
- severe internal damage including a holed sump or cylinder block.

It also records an engineering change: main-bearing crankshaft run-out tolerance was reduced from **50 microns to 30 microns** from engine number **011215-1001200**.

### SSM71816

Warranty documentation supplied to this research expressly references **SSM71816** as an inherent issue in connection with a 2024 failure. Contemporary online reproductions describe SSM71816 as an earlier JLR crankshaft/crankshaft-bearing communication dating from 2014.

Stage 1 does not rely on secondary copies for the exact wording. The authentic complete SSM71816 and its supersession history should be obtained directly from JLR/DVSA for Stage 2.

## 2. China

China's official defect authority recorded a recall of **68,828 imported Land Rover vehicles**.

The official record describes:

- crankshaft assembly/manufacturing-process issues;
- possible insufficient crankshaft-bearing lubrication;
- premature bearing wear;
- possible crankshaft fracture in extreme cases;
- interruption of engine power and safety risk.

The remedy included inspection and **free replacement with an “improved engine”** where potential risk was identified.

The same record states:

- **223** defect reports/complaints;
- **1,338** warranty/claim cases.

### Question created

What engineering changes made the Chinese replacement engine “improved”, and were those same changes incorporated into UK service/remanufactured engines?

## 3. South Korea

South Korea's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport undertook recall action concerning JLR diesel vehicles in 2018, followed by further recall/re-recall activity in 2019.

The investigation must establish:

- the complete affected VIN/production ranges;
- engine specification;
- stated root cause;
- remedy;
- whether equivalent UK vehicles used materially the same crankshaft, bearing and lubrication architecture.

## 4. DVSA and the UK record

DVSA has confirmed directly in FOIA correspondence that it holds responsive information and identified **more than 600 documents that may be relevant** to the wider request.

DVSA states that the material includes:

- customer communications;
- information supplied by manufacturers;
- recall-related material.

This does **not** mean 600 confirmed cases of one defect. It means a substantial regulatory record exists and can be targeted more precisely.

DVSA's own Vehicle Safety Defects and Recalls Code requires investigation of root cause, recurrence, safety consequences and relevant comparable problems elsewhere in the world. The Code also contemplates notification where a safety defect exists overseas that could affect the UK market and an explanation where equivalent UK recall action is not undertaken.

### Critical Stage 2 request

All records in which JLR explained to DVSA why Chinese and/or South Korean crankshaft/bearing recall action did not result in equivalent UK action.

## 5. Primary case: three vehicles, seven failures

The primary case informing this research involves:

**3 separate JLR vehicles**  
**7 engine failures**

Supplied warranty documentation records repeated findings including:

- engine seizure;
- crankshaft failure;
- bottom-end knocking;
- metallic swarf/metal fragments;
- engine replacement recommendations.

One assessment expressly associates a 2024 failure with an **inherent issue referenced as SSM71816**.

The seven-failure history should be completed as a vehicle-by-vehicle chronology for Stage 2.

## 6. Extended warranty question

The supplied warranty review raises a wider question about how known or manufacturer-recognised failure modes are treated within manufacturer-branded extended-warranty products.

Stage 2 should seek:

- claim volume;
- acceptance/rejection rate;
- use of inherent-defect exclusions;
- references to SSM71816, SSM72578 or related communications;
- repeat claims involving replacement/remanufactured engines.

## 7. Remanufactured replacement engines

JLR correspondence supplied for this research confirms that a relevant replacement engine is **remanufactured** and that **no brand-new complete engine assembly is available**.

Land Rover UK separately states that genuine remanufactured parts are rebuilt to new-part specifications/tolerances and incorporate the latest design improvements wherever possible.

### Central question

Exactly what crankshaft, bearing, lubrication, block, oil-pump, tolerance or assembly changes are incorporated in the relevant service/remanufactured engine?

## 8. Consumer cost

Authorised retailer replacement work can reach approximately **£20,000 or more** in affected cases. Long-running public records contain multiple five-figure replacement-engine quotations.

The Stage 2 consumer study should collect actual quotations and separate:

- engine assembly;
- ancillary components;
- labour;
- VAT;
- remanufactured/core exchange status;
- warranty supplied.

## 9. Independent specialist signal

Independent JLR engine businesses openly market dedicated 3.0 TDV6/SDV6 crankshaft and bottom-end repair/rebuild services. Some state that they have handled hundreds of relevant engines or very large volumes of JLR engine work.

This supports the existence of a sustained repair market but does **not yet establish a national prevalence figure**.

Stage 2 should convert existing specialist contacts into structured, anonymised counts supported by workshop records.

## 10. Online research leads

Owner communities, buying guides and specialist sites repeatedly raise:

- failures at a wide range of mileages;
- repeat failure of replacement engines;
- bearing-shell movement/spin;
- crankshaft seizure/fracture;
- lubrication/oil-system hypotheses;
- alleged later design changes;
- high replacement costs.

These are research leads, not proof. Their role is to identify what manufacturer and regulator datasets should be requested and tested.

## 11. What Stage 1 strongly supports

1. JLR formally documented the 3.0 TDV6 crankshaft/bearing failure category.
2. JLR recorded a measurable crankshaft tolerance revision.
3. China undertook a large recall for a closely related crankshaft/bearing-lubrication failure mechanism and used an “improved engine” remedy.
4. South Korea undertook recall and further recall action concerning related JLR diesel failures.
5. DVSA holds a substantial potentially relevant documentary record.
6. The primary case contains repeated failures across multiple vehicles and engines.
7. Warranty material expressly references SSM71816 in characterising an inherent issue.
8. The relevant replacement engine is remanufactured rather than a newly manufactured complete assembly.

## 12. What Stage 1 does not establish

Stage 1 does not establish:

- that every 3.0 TDV6/SDV6 is defective;
- the UK national failure rate;
- that all failures have one root cause;
- that all remanufactured engines lack corrective changes;
- that overseas and UK vehicles are technically identical;
- deliberate concealment by JLR;
- knowing regulatory inaction by DVSA.

Those are matters for evidence, not assumptions.
