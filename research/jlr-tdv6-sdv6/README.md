# JLR 3.0 TDV6/SDV6 Catastrophic Engine Failures

## Stage 1 public-interest research hub

**Status:** Preliminary evidence review and open call for contributions  
**Version:** Stage 1 / 17 August 2026  
**Purpose:** Establish a transparent evidence base, invite scrutiny, and prepare a stronger Stage 2 investigation.

> This project does not assume that every 3.0 TDV6/SDV6 engine is defective, does not state a national failure rate, and does not make a finding of legal wrongdoing. It documents what is currently known, what remains uncertain, and what evidence would answer the unresolved questions.

## Core research question

**Was there a recurring catastrophic crankshaft/main-bearing failure mechanism within sections of the JLR 3.0 TDV6/SDV6 engine population; was it adequately corrected; and was the UK response proportionate when compared with overseas regulatory action?**

## Stage 1 headline findings

- Jaguar Land Rover Special Service Message **SSM72578** formally documents 3.0 TDV6 crankshaft/crankshaft-bearing failure and identifies **rotation of main bearing shells**.
- The same JLR technical message records a reduction in maximum main-bearing crankshaft run-out tolerance from **50 microns to 30 microns** from a specified engine number.
- China's official defect authority recalled **68,828** affected JLR vehicles. The published record refers to crankshaft assembly/manufacturing-process issues, inadequate crankshaft-bearing lubrication, premature wear, possible loss of engine power, and replacement with an **"improved engine"** where risk was identified.
- The Chinese record reports **223 defect reports/complaints** and **1,338 warranty/claim cases**.
- South Korea undertook recall action in 2018 and later further recall activity concerning JLR diesel engines.
- DVSA has confirmed in direct FOIA correspondence that it identified **more than 600 documents that may be relevant** to the wider request, including customer communications and manufacturer information.
- The primary case informing this research involves **three separate JLR vehicles and seven engine failures**.
- Warranty material supplied to the research records repeated seizure/crankshaft failure, bottom-end knocking and metallic contamination. One assessment expressly refers to an **inherent issue referenced as SSM71816**.
- JLR has confirmed in direct correspondence that a relevant replacement engine is **remanufactured** and that no brand-new complete engine assembly is available.
- Independent UK engine specialists describe a substantial and longstanding TDV6/SDV6 crankshaft and bearing repair market. Stage 1 treats these reports as professional signals that require structured quantification rather than as a national prevalence estimate.

## Why this is public

The purpose of Stage 1 is to make the investigation **open to correction, challenge and contribution**. Evidence that contradicts the preliminary interpretation is welcome and will be treated on the same basis as supporting evidence.

We invite contributions from:

- affected owners;
- independent engine specialists and garages;
- current or former JLR staff;
- JLR authorised retailers;
- automotive engineers and academics;
- warranty providers;
- DVSA, DfT and other regulators;
- journalists and researchers.

## Evidence standard

Stage 1 uses four broad evidence levels:

| Level | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| A | Primary documentary evidence | regulator records, JLR technical documents, original correspondence, warranty decisions |
| B | Corroborated professional evidence | engineer reports, garage records, teardown findings |
| C | Structured owner evidence | dated cases supported by invoices, diagnosis, mileage and vehicle identifiers |
| D | Indicative/open-source material | forums, buying guides, media and social posts used to identify leads |

**Level D material is not used alone to establish prevalence or causation.**

## Stage 2 objective

Stage 2 will seek to establish:

1. the technical failure mechanism or mechanisms;
2. the engineering change history;
3. the UK failure population and repeat-failure rate;
4. the specification of JLR service/remanufactured engines;
5. the engineering meaning of the Chinese "improved engine" remedy;
6. whether UK vehicles were technically equivalent to vehicles recalled overseas;
7. what JLR and DVSA knew, and when;
8. whether further safety, regulatory or consumer-remediation action is warranted.

## Research position

This repository is intended to become a transparent, versioned record. Stage 1 will not be silently rewritten as new information arrives. Material corrections will be recorded through Git history and later findings will be published as new versions or as Stage 2.
