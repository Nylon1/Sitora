# Methodology

## 1. Research design

Stage 1 is a documentary and open-source public-interest investigation. It is designed to identify reproducible facts, distinguish them from unresolved hypotheses, and define the evidence required for a more conclusive Stage 2.

The research does **not** treat frequency of online discussion as a substitute for prevalence data and does **not** assume that all reported engine failures share the same root cause.

## 2. Scope

The principal scope is catastrophic bottom-end engine failure involving the JLR 3.0 TDV6/SDV6 diesel family, including potential relationships between:

- crankshaft failure;
- main-bearing-shell movement or rotation;
- bearing wear or seizure;
- lubrication loss or deterioration;
- metallic contamination;
- sudden loss of motive power;
- complete engine replacement;
- repeat failure of service/remanufactured engines.

Separate failure modes are not merged unless the evidence supports a technical relationship.

## 3. Source hierarchy

### Level A - primary evidence

Highest evidential weight:

- official regulator recall records;
- manufacturer technical communications;
- original JLR correspondence;
- original DVSA correspondence;
- original warranty decisions;
- retailer invoices and quotations;
- engineering inspection records.

### Level B - professional corroboration

- independent engineer reports;
- workshop records;
- specialist teardown findings;
- anonymised repair datasets;
- professional statements capable of being checked.

### Level C - structured owner cases

Owner cases are more useful where supported by:

- vehicle model/year;
- partial VIN or registration;
- engine number where available;
- date and mileage at failure;
- service history;
- diagnosis;
- invoice/quotation;
- photographs or technical findings.

### Level D - open-source signals

Forums, buying guides, press reports, specialist marketing pages and social-media posts are used to:

- identify recurring themes;
- locate potential witnesses;
- identify possible engineering mechanisms;
- formulate questions for primary-source investigation.

They are **not used alone to establish a national failure rate, technical causation or legal responsibility**.

## 4. Verification rule

A major factual proposition is classified as established at Stage 1 only where it is supported by:

- a primary source; or
- multiple independent sources whose underlying facts can be checked.

Where the evidence is incomplete, the paper states the issue as an **open question** rather than presenting the suspected answer as fact.

## 5. International comparison method

International recall action is compared using:

1. engine family and technical description;
2. model and production years;
3. stated defect mechanism;
4. safety consequence;
5. remedy;
6. affected population;
7. whether equivalent UK vehicles can be shown to have materially the same specification.

A common model name alone is not treated as proof of technical equivalence.

## 6. Primary case study

The primary case contains three separate JLR vehicles and seven engine failures. It is used as a detailed case study, not as a prevalence estimate.

The research will map each failure by:

- vehicle;
- engine provenance;
- mileage;
- symptoms;
- diagnosis;
- replacement part number;
- warranty response;
- subsequent engine life.

## 7. Open-source research discipline

For online material, each research lead should answer three questions:

1. **What does this source suggest?**
2. **Why is it not proof?**
3. **What testable Stage 2 question does it create?**

This prevents repetition of internet claims from being mistaken for verification.

## 8. Quantification rules

Stage 1 does not currently state a UK prevalence figure. Stage 2 should build a de-duplicated minimum case count using:

- JLR warranty/goodwill data;
- warranty administrator data;
- DVSA complaints;
- independent garage work orders;
- owner submissions;
- replacement-engine records.

Cases must be de-duplicated and classified by confirmed failure mode before aggregation.

## 9. Limitations

Current limitations include:

- incomplete manufacturer engineering-change records;
- incomplete DVSA disclosure;
- absence of audited national denominator data;
- potential self-selection in owner and garage evidence;
- uncertainty over technical equivalence between some international and UK vehicles;
- incomplete provenance of some replacement engines;
- lack of independent teardown/metallurgical testing across a representative sample.

## 10. Ethics and privacy

Public submissions should avoid unnecessary personal information. Full VINs, addresses, telephone numbers, financial details and private correspondence should be redacted before public posting unless specifically required and appropriate.

Evidence can be summarised publicly while original documents are retained privately for verification.

## 11. Reproducibility and corrections

All material changes to the public research text should be versioned in Git. Corrections should explain:

- what changed;
- why it changed;
- the source supporting the correction.

Contrary evidence is expressly welcome.
