# Source Register

This register separates **primary evidence** from **supporting/open-source material**.

## A. Primary manufacturer and regulator sources

### Jaguar Land Rover / NHTSA

**SSM72578 - Crankshaft/Crankshaft Bearing Concerns - 3.0 TDV6 Diesel Engine**  
Jaguar Land Rover, 5 January 2016. Preserved in the US NHTSA technical-bulletin archive.  
https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/SB-10106102-9340.pdf

Research relevance:

- formal JLR technical communication;
- crankshaft/crankshaft-bearing failure;
- rotation of main-bearing shells;
- seizure/metallic debris/knocking/oil-pressure warning;
- reduction in crankshaft main-bearing run-out tolerance from 50 μm to 30 μm.

### China - official defect recall authority

**Jaguar Land Rover China recall notice - 68,828 vehicles**  
State Administration for Market Regulation / Defective Product Recall Technical Centre, 28 December 2018.  
https://www.samrdprc.org.cn/qczh/qczhgg1/201812/t20181229_81245.html

Research relevance:

- 68,828 affected vehicles;
- crankshaft assembly/manufacturing process concerns;
- insufficient crankshaft-bearing lubrication and premature wear;
- possible crankshaft fracture / interruption of engine power;
- inspection and free replacement with an “improved engine” where risk was identified;
- 223 defect reports/complaints;
- 1,338 warranty/claim cases.

### United Kingdom - DVSA

**Vehicle Safety Defects and Recalls: Code of Practice**  
Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency, updated 6 March 2024.  
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/code-of-practice-on-vehicle-safety-defects-and-recalls/vehicle-safety-defects-and-recalls-code-of-practice

### Canada - regulatory comparator

**Transport Canada Recall 2015072 - Land Rover**  
https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/alert-recall/transport-canada-recall-2015072-land-rover

## E. Source policy

When adding a source, identify:

1. publisher / author;
2. date where available;
3. title;
4. stable URL;
5. evidence level;
6. exact factual proposition it supports;
7. any limitations.

Do not cite an online source simply because it repeats a claim. Prefer original regulator, manufacturer or engineering documentation wherever available.
